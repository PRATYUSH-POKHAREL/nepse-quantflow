📖 Quantitative Finance Glossary

Every term you'll encounter in backtesting and quantitative trading, explained simply.

Sharpe Ratio
Measures return per unit of risk. Higher = better risk-adjusted returns. Above 1.0 is good, above 2.0 is excellent.
Max Drawdown
The largest peak-to-trough drop in your portfolio. A 30% drawdown means you lost 30% from your highest point.
Win Rate
Percentage of trades that were profitable. A 40% win rate can still be profitable if winners are bigger than losers.
Moving Average (MA)
Average closing price over a set period. Smooths noise and shows trend direction.
Golden Cross
When a fast MA crosses above a slow MA. Bullish signal — BUY.
Death Cross
When a fast MA crosses below a slow MA. Bearish signal — SELL.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Momentum oscillator (0-100). Above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold.
Bollinger Bands
Volatility bands around a moving average. Price at lower band = potentially oversold. At upper band = potentially overbought.
Backtesting
Testing a strategy on historical data to see how it would have performed.
Slippage
Difference between expected price and actual execution price. Larger orders = more slippage.
Overfitting
When a strategy is too perfectly tuned to past data and fails on future data. The #1 enemy of quants.
Beta
Measures a stock's volatility vs the market. Beta of 1 = moves with market, >1 = more volatile.

📐 Strategy Guides

1. Moving Average Crossover

The simplest trend-following strategy. Uses two moving averages — a fast one and a slow one. When the fast crosses above the slow, you buy. When it crosses below, you sell.

💡 Best for: Trending markets. Worst for: Sideways/choppy markets. Works well on NEPSE banking stocks during bull runs.

2. RSI (Relative Strength Index)

Measures momentum on a 0-100 scale. Buy when RSI crosses above the oversold level (e.g., 35). Sell when it crosses below the overbought level (e.g., 65).

💡 Best for: Range-bound markets. Worst for: Strong trends (can give false signals). Try on NEPSE hydropower stocks.

3. Bollinger Bands

Uses standard deviation to create bands around a moving average. Buy when price touches the lower band (oversold). Sell when price touches the upper band (overbought).

💡 Best for: Mean-reverting stocks. Worst for: Trending markets where price "walks the bands."

4. Buy & Hold

Buy on day 1 and sell on the last day. This is your benchmark. If your strategy can't beat Buy & Hold after costs, it's not worth trading.

📊 Understanding Performance Metrics

Total Return

The percentage your money grew (or shrank). But raw return doesn't tell the full story — you need to know how much risk you took to get it.

Sharpe Ratio — The Most Important Number

Return divided by risk. Think of it as "efficiency." A Sharpe of 2.0 means you got 2 units of return for every 1 unit of risk.

Sharpe RatioRating
Above 2.0🏆 Excellent
1.0 - 2.0✅ Good
0.5 - 1.0⚠️ Acceptable
Below 0.5❌ Poor
Negative💀 Lost money

Max Drawdown — The Pain Metric

Could you handle watching NPR 1,50,000 become NPR 1,00,000? That's a 33% drawdown. Most people panic and sell at the bottom. Know your pain tolerance.

Win Rate — Don't Obsess Over It

A strategy with 30% win rate can be extremely profitable if winners are 5x larger than losers. Focus on expectancy, not win rate.

⚠️ Risk Management

🔴 Golden Rule: Never risk more than you can afford to lose. Backtesting is a learning tool, not a guarantee of future profits.

Position Sizing

Don't put all your capital in one trade. In our backtester, we use 95% for simplicity, but real traders risk 1-5% per trade.

Transaction Costs in Nepal

NEPSE costs are significant:

  • Broker commission: ~0.4% per trade
  • SEBON fee: 0.015%
  • DP charge: NPR 25 flat
  • Round-trip cost: ~0.8-1%

Your strategy must beat ~1% per trade just to break even. This kills most high-frequency strategies.

Market Risk

NEPSE is small and can be volatile. Political events, liquidity crises, and regulatory changes can cause sudden moves that no backtest can predict.

❌ Common Mistakes Beginners Make

1. Overfitting — Testing 1000 parameter combinations and picking the best one. It worked on past data but will fail on future data. Use the optimizer as a guide, not gospel.
2. Ignoring Transaction Costs — A strategy showing 15% returns might actually lose money after NEPSE fees. Always check if the strategy is profitable after costs.
3. Survivorship Bias — Testing only stocks that exist today. What about stocks that went bankrupt? They're not in your data, making results look better than reality.
4. Look-Ahead Bias — Accidentally using future data in your backtest. Our engine prevents this by feeding data bar-by-bar.
5. Ignoring Market Context — A strategy tested during a bull market (2020-2021) may fail in a bear market (2022-2023). Test across different market conditions.

🇳🇵 About NEPSE

Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) is Nepal's only stock exchange, established in 1993 and operating from Kathmandu.

Key Facts

  • Trading Hours: Sunday to Thursday, 11:00 AM - 3:00 PM NPT
  • Listed Companies: 200+ across banking, hydropower, insurance, manufacturing, etc.
  • Circuit Breakers: ±10% for most stocks (stops trading if price moves beyond)
  • Settlement: T+2 (trade date + 2 days)
  • Regulator: SEBON (Securities Board of Nepal)

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this platform free?

Yes. NEPSE QuantFlow is a free educational tool for learning quantitative trading.

Can I use these strategies with real money?

This platform is for education only. Past performance does not guarantee future results. Always consult a financial advisor before investing real money.

Why does my strategy lose money after costs?

NEPSE transaction costs (~1% round-trip) are high. Strategies that make many small trades get eaten by fees. Try longer holding periods.

Where does the data come from?

We attempt to use Yahoo Finance for real NEPSE data. If unavailable, we generate realistic synthetic data for demonstration purposes.

What's the best strategy?

There is no "best" strategy. Every strategy works in some market conditions and fails in others. The key is understanding when a strategy works and managing risk.
